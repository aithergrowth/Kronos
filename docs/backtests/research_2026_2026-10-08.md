# Research 2026 (r26): report for Max, 8 October 2026

**Scope.** Profile variants of the four live markets (EURUSD, gold XAUUSD, NAS100, BTCUSD) run on January-September 2026
only, FTMO costs (spread + commission) applied, judged against the live base of each market. 142 variant runs in all
(including the 3-year control runs). The bar a variant had to clear: more R *and* a higher win rate, on more than one or
two trades, in both halves of the year (Jan-Apr and May-Sep), without a cliff in the parameter, and surviving two
independent verifiers.

**Outcome in one line: no variant survived on any market. The four profiles stay as they run since 8 October.**

Two things to keep in mind when reading the tables:

1. The `r26_<SYM>_base` row is the live base *as it stood on the morning of 8 October* (the q133 "w9" candidate of each
   market with `confirmation.poi_timeframes: [4H, 1H]`). At 14:59 that day (commit f57dcc4) two r26 results were taken
   live: EURUSD `bias.no_trade_against: [1W, 4H]` + `bias.shift_flips_balance: true` (= run `r26_EURUSD_shift_vetow4h`)
   and gold `structure.poi_gap_choice: last` + `confirmation.allow_retest: true` (= run `r26_XAUUSD_cmb_a`). NAS100 and
   BTC run the base. So the honest "before" of every proposal is the **LIVE** row, not the base row; the dR column in the
   tables is against the base.
2. Every number is a handful of trades: 11-39 per market in nine months. One trade is one R. The "top3" column (the share
   of the year's R that the best three trades carry) says how thin the rest is: on EURUSD the best three trades are more
   than the whole year (the other trades net a loss), on BTC they are 91 %.

## 1. Every variant run, per market

Columns: trades / won % / R / R a trade / dd (worst drawdown in R) / Jan-Apr and May-Sep R / top3 (share of R in the best
three trades) / dR against the base / trades added and removed against the base / verdict. "Same ledger" means the
override changed nothing beyond what its sibling already changed. Rows ending in `_3y` are the 3-year control runs
(February 2024 - September 2026); the table shows their 2026 window (identical to the 2026 run), the 3-year numbers are in
the verdict and in section 5.

Meaning of the overridden keys (from `kronos_trader/config.py`):

- `bias.no_trade_against`: timeframes that veto a match when they read the opposite direction. `bias.shift_flips_balance`:
  the balance view turns when a close goes beyond the far edge of the last gap (the balance shift). `bias.conflict_rule`:
  what a timeframe reads when its liquidity view and balance view disagree (neutral = 50/50, recent = the newer one).
  `bias.full_combos` / `min_matching_timeframes`: the allowed timeframe combinations and how many must agree.
  `bias.scalp_enabled`: the 1D+4H+1H scalp combination counts as a match.
- `structure.poi_break_window`: the displacement must close through X within this many candles after candle 3 (0 = at
  once, -1 = any time). `structure.poi_extent`: how far a zone reaches from P (liquidity = to X; gap = to the gap;
  protector = to the protecting swing). `structure.poi_gap_choice`: with several gaps in one impulse, first = the earliest,
  last = the one nearest the break. `structure.min_gap_fraction` / `gap_median_window`: how big a gap must be to count as a
  balance level. `structure.equal_level_tolerance_pct`: equal highs/lows clustering. `structure.swing_left/right`: fractal
  width of a swing. `structure.lookback_by_timeframe`: how far back the structure is read.
- `confirmation.bs_threshold`: gap_edge = the shift closes beyond the opposing gap; protector = beyond the candle that
  caused it. `bs_requires_structure` / `bs_requires_sweep`: a shift counts only after a structure break / a liquidity
  sweep on the entry timeframe. `search_from_reentry`: the confirmation search starts at price's latest entry into the
  zone. `opposing_gap_lookback`: how far before the touch the opposing gap may have formed. `allow_retest`: a second visit
  to a zone is traded. `confirmation_tf_mode`: exact = one entry timeframe per zone timeframe, at_least = that one and the
  ones between. `max_adverse_move_*`: no entry after a long run against the trade. `max_touch_age_hours`: older zones are
  not traded. `poi_in_poi`: only zones inside a higher-timeframe zone.
- `risk.max_entry_depth`: the entry may lie at most this fraction of the zone inside it (0 = off). `risk.max_entry_outside`:
  the shift's close may lie at most this fraction of the zone height outside it (0 = off). `risk.limit_entry_fraction`
  / `limit_entry_minutes`: a limit order this fraction of the way from the signal back to the stop, valid this long (0 =
  market entry). `risk.tp_policy`: liquidity (nearest liquidity), pullback_origin (origin of the move), previous_extreme.

## 4. What needs code (short specs, from the curator)

1. **`conf_lim_break_level`: a limit order resting on the balance level the shift closed through** (BTC first, then gold).
   `RiskParams.limit_entry_at: str = ""` ("" = fraction as now; "break_level" = the limit rests at
   `confirmation.break_level`, the opposing gap edge the 5m shift closed through, capped so it never lies beyond the stop
   and never above the signal price for a long / below it for a short). `backtest/runner.py`: when `limit_entry_at ==
   "break_level"`, `pending["limit"] = spec.round_price(setup.confirmation.break_level)` instead of `price_now - sign * frac
   * |price_now - stop|`; the same fill / expiry / target-first handling; sizing on the smaller stop. `live.py`: the same
   branch where the live runner places its limit; ledger meta `entry_mode = "limit break_level"`. Yaml: `risk.limit_entry_at:
   break_level` with `risk.limit_entry_minutes: 240`. Why: Dorus's entry after the shift is the retest of the balance level
   the shift closed through, not a fraction of the stop distance. The BTC ladder shows the fill price is where the R is
   (market +46.3R, 10 % +49.4, 15 % +52.4, 25 % +58.1) while every deeper fraction misses winners that never retrace (3 of
   19 at 15 %). Gold's 10 % fraction gave +0.6R at 48 % won, so gold needs a level-based fill or none. To be measured on
   BTC and gold once built; expected R a trade between the 15 % and 25 % fractions with fewer arbitrary misses.
2. **`conflict_rule_by_timeframe`** (EURUSD, optional, not worth building now). `BiasParams.conflict_rule_by_timeframe:
   Dict[Timeframe, str] = {}`; in `bias.timeframe_bias` use `rule = params.conflict_rule_by_timeframe.get(
   st.series.timeframe, params.conflict_rule)`; yaml `bias.conflict_rule_by_timeframe: {1M: recent, 1W: recent}`. Why:
   "recent" on the month and week only (Max's remark was about those two), neutral below so the required-1H gate keeps its
   50/50 reading. The recent rule showed no value anywhere in 2026 (EURUSD -7.0R, gold -0.3R with the drawdown doubled,
   NAS100 -5.0R, BTC -1.8R with 47 added trades), and on EURUSD a directional weekly reading vetoes the profitable
   counter-monthly shorts of April-June.

Research tooling, not bot code: gold has no 3-year base ledger that matches today's profile (the `XAUUSD_cand_fix_full`
run predates the gold target change: same 34 trades in 2026, +24.7R against the base's +31.4R), so a gold 3-year control

## 5. Caveats, honest

- **Sample sizes.** 12 / 38 / 16 / 19 live trades in nine months. A one-trade change is +-1R and shows up as a 5-10 %
  swing in the win rate. The curator's bar (more than two trades, both halves) rejected eleven runs that read better on
  paper; every one of them was one or two trades when the ledger was opened. Within a market one trade decides the sign:
  EURUSD's 6 January short (+7.3R) is the entire 4H+1H story; BTC's two June trades carry the limit-entry story.
- **Top-heavy R.** EURUSD base top3 = 128 % (the best three trades are more than the year; the other 13 net -2.1R),
  live 90 %; BTC 91 % (Jan-Apr +1.8R on 8 trades, May-Sep +44.5R); NAS100 54 %; gold 39-49 %. Gold is the only market
  whose 2026 R is spread over many trades.
- **BTC carries one trade from outside the period.** The ledgers run to 2 October and `r26.py eval` has no upper bound: the
  +7.1R BTC trade of 2 October 07:50 is in every BTC row and in the funded replay. January-September only, BTC reads
  18 trades, 61 %, +39.2R, May-Sep +37.4, top3 97%: 97 % of its R in three trades, +1.8R in the first four months. The verdicts hold either way (every
  difference between BTC runs sits inside January-September), but "BTC +46.3R in 2026" overstates the period by 7R.
- **Halves are unequal.** Jan-Apr is four months, May-Sep five (BTC's includes 2 October). Many "better" runs are better in
  one half and worse in the other; the table marks them. Nothing in 2026 is out of sample for any of these settings: the
  base itself was chosen on 2024-2026 data.
- **The 3-year controls run so far.** EURUSD: live-equivalent 49 trades, 47 %, +45.7R, dd -5.6 (2024 20 tr 35 % +8.7R,
  2025 17 tr 59 % +24.1R, 2026 12 tr 50 % +12.9R) against the morning base 54, 39 %, +40.9R, dd -10.4; the candidate cap
  46, 46 %, +38.0R; the cap alone 49, 39 %, +37.1R; `extent_prot` 45, 40 %, +41.8R. BTC: `extent_prot` 83 trades, 63 %,
  +73.0R, dd -4.0 against the base 91, 59 %, +68.6R, dd -5.3. Gold: `extent_prot` 118 trades, 50 %, +80.2R (2024 46 tr
  39 % +14.8R, 2025 42 tr 50 % +28.2R, 2026 30 tr 67 % +37.2R) with no valid base to compare (above). EURUSD 2024 reads
  29-35 % won on every variant: the 2026 win rate of 50 % is one good year.
- **Two leads the curator did not carry, with their numbers (not proposed, listed so they are not lost):**
  BTC `structure.poi_extent: protector` 2026 +2.1R, 71 %, both halves (+1.1 / +1.0) on 1 in / 3 out / 1 re-priced (the 5
  May stop-out becomes a +0.67R target with the wider zone's stop), and over three years +4.4R, 63 vs 59 % won, dd -4.0 vs
